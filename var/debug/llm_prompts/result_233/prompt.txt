VibCamera: mmWave and Camera Fusion for
Multi-point Vibration Monitoring
Xiulong Liu, Zhihua Yang, Hankai Liu, Xin Xie, Xinyu Tong

Abstract—As a diagnostic method of equipment operational
status, vibration monitoring plays a signiﬁcant role in industrial
systems. It is necessary to monitor multiple equipment com-
ponents simultaneously, due to their different vibration modes.
Previous solutions either work in an invasive manner or face
challenges in object localization and results correspondence.
Therefore, we propose VibCamera, a vibration monitoring system
that combines mmWave radar and computer vision technology.
We propose an expand-shrink method to optimize object detec-
tion results of computer vision and combine camera localization
results to extract mmWave signals. Additionally, we employ
mmWave data recombination and respective ﬁtting methods to
calculate the vibration characteristics for each point accurately.
The experiment shows that after fusing visual information, the
target detection accuracy is improved to 94.8%, and the cluster
point efﬁciency is improved by 23.3%. Furthermore, amplitude
and frequency measurement errors are reduced to 29.1μm and
0.08Hz, respectively.

I. I NTRODUCTION
A. Motivation and Problem Statement
Industrial equipment faults such as screw detachment and
connection misalignment can manifest in abnormal vibration
amplitude and frequency. By monitoring the equipment vibra-
tion, potential faults can be promptly detected, enabling timely
repair and maintenance to prevent accidents and mitigate po-
tential losses. V ariations in work modes and states may differ
in the vibration characteristics of different equipment parts.
Therefore, performing simultaneous vibration measurements at
multiple parts is crucial for effective equipment fault diagnosis.
Traditional measurement solutions based on Inertial Mea-
surement Unit (IMU) [1] or laser [2] are more suitable for
single-point measurement. Camera-based methods [3], [4] can
distinguish equipment parts, while it requires proximity to the
target, making it challenging to achieve multi-object detection
at longer distances in industrial environments. Therefore, we
consider using the wireless signal, which is contactless, easy
to deploy, and capable of spatial segmentation.
B. Limitation of Prior Art
Existing wireless signal-based solutions still possess certain
limitations despite their advantages. Wireless systems based
on long-wavelength signals represented by RFID [5], [6]
Correspondence Author: Xin Xie.
have limited displacement sensitivity. The spatial resolution
of WiFi [7] is not sufﬁcient to achieve precise spatial dif-
ferentiation and multi-point vibration measurement. Although
Ultra-Wideband (UWB) radar [8], [9] can achieve multi-target
vibration detection and distinction in the range domain, its
subsequent spatial spectrum estimation ability is limited, and
antenna array deployment is also difﬁcult.
In recent years, many solutions leveraging millimeter-wave
(mmWave) radar have been proposed, beneﬁting from its high
spatial resolution and displacement sensitivity [10]. Position-
ing the vibration source is the primary and crucial step in
vibration measurement. mmVib [11] enhances displacement
sensitivity through chirp decomposition and demonstrates
the feasibility of utilizing mmWave radar for precise vi-
bration measurement. However, range-based signal extraction
constrains its spatial discrimination and localization ability.
GWaltz [12] performs object detection in terms of distance
and horizontal angle dimensions, but it still cannot ensure
that the detected target matches the interested one. Multi-
Vib [13] installs corner reﬂectors on the object to enhance
the reﬂection intensity of the interested position and extend
the target localization to 3D space. The introduction of corner
reﬂectors increases the deployment cost and complexity.
In summary, existing approaches have two main limitations:
First, there is a lack of association between detected radar
points and real-world targets. Therefore, the system cannot
efﬁciently determine which object the vibration measurement
results belong to. Secondly, the various parts of the object
are not subdivided, making it difﬁcult to conduct all-point
vibration measurements of the object.

of the interested target to the mmWave radar, which extracts
the corresponding signal for vibration monitoring. The Vib-

D. Challenges and Solutions
We need to address the following key challenges when
implementing the VibCamera system.
The ﬁrst challenge is how to provide precise position refer-
ence for mmW ave.Although the semantic segmentation model
can predict categories for each pixel, it may not fully cover
the object or may exceed its actual boundary. Consequently, it
is difﬁcult to achieve comprehensive vibration measurement
in all parts of the target, as there may be omissions or
incorrect predictions. We propose an expand-shrink method
to achieve more precise visual segmentation. This method
involves performing a dilation operation to expand the detected
area, followed by clustering the 3D point positions to remove
outlier points. Afterward, the detection results are utilized
to identify the object’s position, guiding the extraction and
calculation of mmWave signals.
The second challenge is how to extract an accurate phase
curve for vibration measurement.The discontinuity between
radar frames and the changes in the vibration direction can
lead to variations in the phase curve. Therefore, calculating the
amplitude directly from the original phase would yield inaccu-
rate results. We concatenate signals from various frames and
reorganize the radar data into segments based on a consistent
displacement in the same direction. By individually ﬁtting each
reorganized data segment, we can extract the accurate phase
data for vibration measurement.

B. mmW ave Object Positioning
We perform Range-FFT onS(t) in each chirp to convert
the signal into the range domain and determine the target
distance. Next, we perform Doppler-FFT on the Range-FFT
processed signal to detect the velocity information of the
target. After obtaining range and velocity information, the
signal is processed using the 2D Constant False Alarm Rate
(CFAR) algorithm to generate Range-Doppler (RD) point
clouds, as shown in Fig.3. To obtain the spatial position of the
target, we need to estimate the signal direction of arrival. By
performing 2D Angle-FFT on the extracted RD point cloud
signals, we can obtain the azimuth and the elevation of the
target. Finally, through the above steps, the 4D point cloud
can be obtained, which includes information on the position,
velocity, and spatial distribution of the target, providing a more
comprehensive target perception [15].
C. Vision Object Segmentation
Object segmentation, as one of the fundamental applications
of computer vision, aims to determine the object to which
each pixel belongs. Due to its high prediction precision,
we leverage this technology for vibration object recognition
and positioning. The Fully Convolutional Network (FCN)
is a crucial foundation for object segmentation models. By
replacing the fully connected layer with convolutional layers,
FCN can process input images of any size and generate dense
predictions of the same size. Moreover, FCN employs up-
sampling operations to restore high-dimensional feature maps
to the input image size, thereby preserving valuable spatial
information. This capability enables FCN to classify pixels
into different categories and provides essential insights and
groundwork for developing subsequent models, such as the
DeepLabV3 model utilized in this paper.
III. S YSTEM DESIGN
This section introduces the design of VibCamera, which
combines data from the camera and mmWave radar for vi-
bration monitoring. Fig. 4 shows the workﬂow of VibCamera,
which consists of three main modules.
• Target localization: The camera captures the scene’s
RGB image and depth image. By using a trained semantic
segmentation model for prediction and the expand-shrink
algorithm for improving coverage, the area of the target
object can be detected.
• Measurement point selection: By combining the de-
tected positions from the camera with the radar points
generated by the radar, VibCamera selectively chooses
the position points that accurately represent the target.
• Vibration calculation: VibCamera extracts the radar
signal from the selected position points, removes static
components, and then calculates the vibration frequency
and amplitude for each point.

the outlier points. The blue area shown in Fig. 5 represents

method achieves a higher target detection accuracy and more
comprehensive coverage, which is also the foundation of multi-

C. Vibration Calculation
1) Vibration Signal Extraction:The discrete signal can be
represented as points in the IQ domain, with the real part as the
x-axis and the imaginary part as the y-axis. According to the
electromagnetic wave propagation theory, the phase change
of the reﬂected signal is proportional to its displacement
change. Therefore, in an ideal scenario, reciprocating motion
can be represented as an arc centered at the origin in the
IQ domain as shown in Fig. 7(d). The central angle of the
arc θ1 corresponds to the amplitude, and the reciprocating
phase frequency represents the vibration frequency. However,
the center of the arc may deviate from the origin due to
the background reﬂection, which is also known as static
components. The size of the central angle of the shifted arc
changes, as shown as θ1 and θ2, which leads to incorrect
amplitude calculation results. Therefore, it is necessary to ﬁt
the arc, determine its center position, and calculate its central
angle to measure the amplitude accurately.
The circle ﬁtting of the IQ points can be transformed into
an optimization problem [11]. LetX = {xn,c}N×C denote

to solve two problems. The ﬁrst problem is the discontinuity
between frames. For example, there is a break between frame
1’s end and frame 2’s beginning. The second problem is the
phase direction change caused by the change in vibration
direction. The change may occur at the junction of frames,
as indicated by the purple arrows at the junction of frames 2
and 3, or within frames, as indicated by the black arrows in
frame 1. The phase change within the frame divides the arc

it is necessary to concatenate and reorganize the signals to
represent vibration effectively.
Firstly, we need to determine the turning point of the
vibration, which is the turning point in the phase direction,
represented by the black dot on the arc in Fig. 7(c). If we
calculate the phase of the blue arc relative to the origin, the
direction of phase change does not change, which means there
is no turning point. This inconsistency with the actual situation

ﬁtting to ﬁnd the initial circle centerP. By calculating the
phase from the points on the arc to the initial centerP,w e
can accurately determine the position of the phase transition
and identify the turning point.

turning point. The data between two turning points is consid-

as shown in Fig. 10(a), indicate that whether in the corri-
dor environment or the warehouse environment, VibCamera
demonstrates excellent performance within a range of 4m.
This conﬁrms its ability to resist multipath interference and
accurately detect vibrations.
2) Impact of Measurement Angle:In this experiment, we
maintain a frequency of 10Hz, an amplitude of 500μm, and a
distance of 2m. We consider the angle between the midpoint of
the radar RXs and the accelerometer as the measurement angle
and set it from 0◦ to 30◦. Fig. 10(b) shows that VibCamera has
exhibited excellent measurement accuracy and anti-multipath
capabilities within a 30◦ angle range. Overall, in the warehouse
environment, as the angle increases, the error in vibration
amplitude gradually increases. The angle range of the depth
camera limits the effective angle range of the system.
3) Impact of Measurement Point Position:Since we mea-
sure the multi-point vibration, we need to evaluate the effect
of measurement positions. As shown in Fig. 8(b), we conduct
experiments on the positions of four points ona, b, c, and d.
We maintain a frequency of 10Hz, an amplitude of 500μm,
and a distance of 2m. Fig. 10(c) shows that VibCamera shows
good accuracy for different parts. The average amplitude
error in the corridor environment and warehouse environment
is 19.38μm and 23.56μm. The measurement effect is very
stable, which lays the foundation for comprehensive vibration
monitoring of multiple objects.
4) Impact of Vibration Amplitude:In this experiment, we
keep the frequency to 10Hz and the distance to 2mand change
the amplitudes from 200μmto 1mm. Fig. 10(d) illustrates that
in the corridor environment, there is no signiﬁcant amplitude
error across all vibration amplitudes. This is attributed to
the utilization of the camera to extract the signal from the
radar, effectively mitigating interference from non-target radar
points reﬂected by the environment. The smaller the amplitude,
the more difﬁcult it is to measure the amplitude. The larger
the amplitude, the wider the error range. Therefore, the best
detection effect is achieved at 400μm.
V. RELA TEDWORK
A. Vibration Measurement
In traditional vibration measurement methods, the IMU [1]
requires to be directly deployed on the object, which may in-
troduce extra deployment and maintenance costs. Laser sensor
[2], [16] has the ability to detect micrometer-level vibrations
but is more suitable for single-point vibration detection. High-
deﬁnition camera [3], [4] needs appropriate lighting and a
very close distance to measure the subtle vibration. RF-based
methods have shown great potential in vibration measurement.
RFID [5], [6] is limited in detecting small amplitudes due to
its long wavelength. UWHear [9] uses IR-UWB technology
to distinguish sound sources through some light-building ma-
terials. ART [7] uses 2.4GHz WiFi to detect sub-millimeter
vibration and recover high-quality audio.
B. mmW ave-based Measurement
mmWave has a higher resolution for vibration measure-
ment due to its mm-level wavelength [11], [13], [17]–[20].
Mikhelson et al. [17] analyze vibration signals in the IQ
domain and proposed the ﬁtting method to remove static
components. Biet al. [18] introduce a digital phase shifter for
multi-arc circle ﬁtting to improve signal processing accuracy.
mmVib [11] provides a signal processing method based on
the multi-chirp property to achieve the micrometer-level vi-
bration measurement, but it treats the entire object as a source

of vibration. Multi-Vib [13] achieves multi-point vibration
monitoring by installing the designed corner reﬂectors to the
object, which introduces deployment difﬁculty. Besides, the
calculation results lack directionality, making it difﬁcult to
directly determine which label the calculated signal comes
from in a multiple-label environment.
C. Multimodal Combination
The integration with vision enhances the universality and
reliability of mmWave sensing. VPBCT [21] infers the optimal
beam pair for transceivers based on the vision-measured vehi-
cle position to improve communication performance. Liet al.
[22] distinguish pedestrian or humanoid signs on the road by
calculating the radar cross-section of mmWave, making up for
the shortcomings of computer vision. Wanget al. [23] combine
the train speed calculated by the mmWave and the loop closure
detection result of camera data to accurately locate the train.
ReID [24] achieves cross-modal human re-identiﬁcation by
analyzing human gait data from vision and mmWave radar.
VI. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this paper, we present VibCamera to locate and measure
the vibration of objects accurately. VibCamera introduces com-
puter vision technology into mmWave vibration measurement,
achieving more accurate target detection, localization, and
differentiation. To improve the model prediction result of the
target, we propose an expand-shrink algorithm to expand the
pixels of the target object more reasonably. To eliminate non-
object points, we use the visual range of the target to ﬁlter
the radar point cloud. We solve the engineering problem of
vibration extraction through radar data reorganization and re-
spective ﬁtting and conduct more accurate vibration character-
istic calculations. Experimental results reveal that VibCamera
can accurately measure the vibration characteristics of points,
with the average error of vibration frequency and amplitude
as 29.1μm and 0.08Hz.
In the future, we will further enhance the practicality and
robustness of VibCamera. We plan to eliminate the dependence
on the depth camera and use low-cost RGB cameras to achieve
determination assistance for measurement points. Besides, we
will attempt to deploy VibCamera on robots to facilitate
automated, omnidirectional device vibration monitoring.
