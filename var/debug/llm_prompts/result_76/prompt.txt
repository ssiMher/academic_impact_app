Visual-Based Out-of-Plane Rotation Measurement
Using 3-D Moir ´e-Based Marker
Mingzhu Zhu
 , Maocan Wu
 , Mingxuan Wei
 , Bingwei He
 , Jiantao Liu
 , and Junzhi Yu
 , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—This article proposes a novel 3-D Moir ´e-based visual
marker with an explicit geometric model. The proposed marker is
designed for out-of-plane rotation measurement, which comprises
two periodic masks etched on the opposite sides of a glass
wafer. The masks project a Moir ´e pattern on the image plane
of the observing camera, and this Moir ´e pattern is dramatically
sensitive to out-of-plane rotation. The key contribution is that we
ﬁrst explicitly derive, for the ﬁrst time, the geometric relationship
between the rotation angle and the Moir ´e pattern to build a
straightforward measurement model, which reveals that the angle
is simply determined by the Moir ´e phase at the image principal
point. The accuracy is independent of the observation distance
and camera intrinsic parameters. Estimation and calibration
algorithms are given. Experiment demonstrates the superiority,
which shows an accuracy that is up to 7 times higher than
traditional visual markers and at least 2 times higher than state-
of-the-art 3-D Moir ´e-based markers.
Index Terms—3-D Moir ´e eﬀect, out-of-plane rotation measure-
ment, visual encoder, visual marker, visual-based measurement.

II. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the proposed system consists of a
camera and a marker. The marker is a thin glass wafer with
size W × H and thickness s. The marker coordinate system
Om is deﬁned at the bottom-left corner of the glass’s front
side, with the X- and Y-axes aligned with the horizontal and
vertical edges, respectively. The camera coordinate system O c
is deﬁned at the optical center, and the X- and Y-axes are
parallel to the sensor’s edges. The Z-axes of both O m and Oc
are facing the direction of the optical axis. Two masks are
etched on the opposite sides of the wafer, whose transmission
maps are denoted as Ti, where i = 1,2 represent the front
and back ones, respectively. In this article, Ti is periodic and
chessboard-like.
An out-of-plane rotation angle θ can be described by the
two included angles between the Z-axis of O c and X- and

IV. ALGORITHM
This section proposes the algorithm used to solve the out-
of-plane angle θ from the captured image. It is based on the
model described in (19). The image-processing workﬂow is
ﬁrst given with known Aα, Aβ, and Br, and the calibration
process of Aα, Aβ, and Br is introduced later.
A. Estimation
An example of the estimation workﬂow is illustrated in
Fig. 5. The details of each step are given below.
1) Image Undistortion: The lens distortion is cali-
brated [23] and then removed from the input image.
2) Projective Rectiﬁcation: The Moir´e pattern S is cropped
from the image. However, there is a projective distortion
in the observed S . The distortion will impair the subpixel
accuracy of the following steps. Therefore, we rectify the
image so that the viewing angle faces the marker. This
is accomplished by imposing a homography that maps
the four S corners to the four image corners. Note that
the image principal point is shifted in this step (see the
green dot in Fig. 5).
3) Peaks Recovery: The peaks of S are detected by Fourier-
based techniques, as marked by blue circles in Fig. 5.
Then, the peaks are reﬁned by quadric surface ﬁt-
ting [24] to subpixel accuracy, as marked by red crosses
in the middle of Fig. 5.
4) Principal Phase Estimation: The peaks are rectiﬁed by
applying a homography that maps them to a standard
array, where the coordinates of each peak are represented
by two integers, each corresponding to a multiple of 2 π,
as shown in the right of Fig. 5. Then, the homogra-
phy is applied to the principal point. Its coordinates
in the standard array, when divided by 2 π, reveal its
phase within [0 ,2π), denoted as ( ϕ(0),ψ(0)). Then, the
principal phase with period ambiguity is ( Φ(0),Ψ(0)) =
(ϕ(0) + 2mπ,ψ(0) + 2nπ), where m and n are unknown
integers.
5) Rotation Angle Estimation: With known Aα, Aβ, and
Br, the principal phase ( Φ(0),Ψ(0)) is used to estimate
the desired rotation angle θ based on (19). Since the
period ambiguity is carried over, θ has multiple discrete
solutions. Currently, there are two approaches to resolve
this ambiguity. One method relies on the PnP results
from the four S corners, while the other involves intro-
ducing multiple S with diﬀerent frequencies. Both have
some limitations. In this article, we ignore this issue and
focus on the accuracy improvement brought by the 3-D

V. EXPERIMENTS
A. Simulation Test
It can be seen based on (19) that, with a ﬁxed principal
phase error, a larger Br results in a smaller estimation error,
suggesting a higher mask frequency w and a thicker wafer s.
However, the calibration process complicates this relationship;
thus, we investigate the e ﬀect by simulation. For simplicity,
only the e ﬀect on α is considered by setting β = 0, as the
impact on β is identical. The conﬁgurations are listed below.
1) Masks: The initial phases of the masks are trivial, thus
both are set to 0 for concise, as ϕ1 = ϕ2 = ∆ ϕ = 0.
Three diﬀerent frequencies and gaps are tested, as w =
π/20,π/10,π/5 µm−1 and s = 2.5,4.5 mm.
2) Calibration: The angles used for calibration are θCal =
−10◦,−9◦,..., 10◦. Therefore, the N, θ0, and ∆θ in (20)
are 21, −10◦, and 1 ◦, respectively.
3) Measurement: There are 201 samples used for accuracy
testing, including θTst = αTst = −10◦,−9.9◦,..., 10◦.
In both calibration and measurement, given a ground-truth
angle θCal or θTst, we simulate the ground-truth principal phase
ΦGT(0) by (8), and then simulate the estimated principal phase

ϕ(0) by adding an uniform distributed random value δ ranged
in 2 π[−σ,σ], as ϕ(0) = mod((ΦGT(0) + δ)/(2π)). The noisy
estimations ϕ(0) are used in calibration to get the noisy internal
parameters Aα and Br, and then used in measurement to
estimate the rotation angles θ = α. The estimated angles are

The simulation results are displayed in Fig. 6, where the
absolute errors |θ −θTst| of di ﬀerent σ are shown. As can
be seen, the estimation accuracy is positively related to w
and s. It should be noted that in the real world, w and s
cannot be freely chosen, as overly large values induce optical

patterns. This, however, does not a ﬀect the simulation, and in
the real world, it is safe as long as ws < 100π, according to
our experience.
In practice, the principal phase error can be e ﬀectively
controlled to σ< 0.1. Under the conditions of w = π/20 µm−1
and s = 4.5 mm, an estimation error of less than 0.05 ◦ can be
achieved. Subsequent real-world experimental results demon-
strate that this prediction is conservative, as the prototype
can achieve this accuracy with only s = 1 mm. Notably, an
accuracy of 0.05 ◦ is at least twice that of the best-reported
results among existing 3-D Moir ´e-based methods [19], [20].
Furthermore, the entire simulation process does not incor-
porate any information about distance or camera intrinsic
parameters. The principal phase is the sole dominant factor.

C. Comparative Analysis of Existing Methods
The angular errors of several conventional methods and
state-of-the-art Moir ´e-based methods are listed in Table I.
The results of the conventional methods are based on Sec-
tion V-B.3. However, for the three aliasing Moir ´e-based
methods and the two other 3-D Moir ´e-based methods, strict
quantitative comparison is impractical due to customized hard-
ware, the lack of publicly available algorithms, and insuﬃcient
implementation details. Therefore, we directly cite the errors
they reported. These values can only reﬂect an approximate
level of accuracy and should not be regarded as a precise
basis for comparison when the reported errors of two methods
are close.
As shown in Table I, ArUco [25] has an error larger
than 2◦, this is because the subpixel ambiguity of corners [24].
In contrast, methods based on cross points achieve errors in
the range from 0.1 ◦ to 0.2 ◦, whether using PnP or BA. The
aliasing Moir ´e-based methods yield errors in the range from
0.2◦to 2◦. These methods exploit the aliasing e ﬀect that arises
when a camera captures high-frequency patterns, such as the
pixel array of a display, thereby avoiding the use of visually
obtrusive artiﬁcial markers. However, in terms of accuracy,
they oﬀer no advantage over conventional methods.
Tanaka [19] and Qiu et al. [20] also leveraged the 3-D
Moir´e eﬀect to measure out-of-plane rotation angles, referred
to as u-LentiMark and Moir ´eTag, respectively. Despite the
diﬀerent marker designs, the underlying principle is essentially
the same. The key distinction is that our method is based
on an explicit analytical model, whereas u-LentiMark and
Moir´eTag rely on look-up tables or learning-based approaches.
This explicit analytical model enables us to calibrate the
fabrication errors of the markers, uncover and exploit the
strong correlation between principal phase and rotation angle,
and ultimately achieve high accuracy, as u-LentiMark and
Moir´eTag remain within the same range as conventional
methods, our method achieves errors below 0.05 ◦, corre-
sponding to at least a twofold improvement in accuracy.
Nevertheless, the relatively coarse and brute-force approaches
of u-LentiMark and Moir ´eTag have their own advantage.
They can ignore the severe refraction e ﬀect at large angles
and thereby enable a wider measurement range. As a result,
both u-LentiMark and Moir ´eTag achieve ranges more than
twice that of ours. Therefore, our method is more appropriate
for situations where rotation angles are limited but stringent
accuracy is needed.
D. Limitation
The proposed method currently has two limitations. First,
the angular measurement range is small, as −10◦ to 10◦. This
is because the measurement model relies on a small-angle
approximation to circumvent refraction e ﬀects. To extend
the range, we must directly address the refraction issue and
develop a refraction correction algorithm. The second issue
is the lack of a period disambiguation approach. We do
not rely on conventional ﬁducial markers for disambigua-
tion, as done in u-LentiMark, because in our case, the
enhanced accuracy surpasses the resolution limit of such
markers for resolving phase ambiguity. We also avoid the
multifrequency approach used in Moir ´eTag, as it signiﬁcantly
compromises the simplicity and esthetic appeal of the marker.

VI. CONCLUSION
This article presents a novel visual marker that lever-
ages the 3-D Moir ´e e ﬀect for high-precision out-of-plane
rotation measurement. A concise geometric model is derived,
linking the rotation angle to the Moir ´e phase at the
image’s principal point. This enables straightforward and
accurate angle estimation, and the resulting measurements
are inherently invariant to both distance and camera intrinsic
parameters.
Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method
achieves an accuracy of approximately 0.05 ◦. Compared
to ArUco markers commonly used in virtual reality
applications, our method o ﬀers a two-orders-of-magnitude
improvement in accuracy. Compared to cross point mark-
ers widely adopted in visual measurement, the accuracy
improvement is about 5–7-fold. Moreover, our method out-
performs other 3-D Moir ´e-based methods by a factor of
two to three. Real-world experiments demonstrate that the
method is independent of both distance and camera intrinsic
parameters.
This work merely scratches the surface of the potential
of the 3-D Moir ´e e ﬀect in visual measurement. The unique
viewpoint-sensitive property of 3-D Moir ´e patterns suggests
broader possibilities. Future work will focus on extending the
measurable angular range, developing eﬀective disambiguation
strategies, and exploring more applications.
