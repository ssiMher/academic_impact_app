Abstract—Document Presentation Attack is an anti-forensic op-
eration that conceals the forgery traces of image manipulation
in the digital domain. Existing document presentation attack de-
tection (DPAD) methods show unsatisfactory performance under
samples with different contents and qualities. In this work, we focus
on the DPAD task on screen-recapturing channel and exploit the
prior knowledge of distortion (i.e., moiré pattern) in the spectral do-
main to address these limitations. We propose a frequency-domain
moiré augmentation (FMAG) strategy that enhances the spectral
components contributed to the moiré distortion, improving the
generalization performance under different document contents.
We devise the mask moiré frequency modeling (M 2 FM) scheme to
reconstruct the moiré-related spectral components in low-quality
samples under the guidance of the spectral distortion model and a
pre-trained DPAD classiﬁer. To evaluate the generalization per-
formance, we collect the diverse Screen Recaptured Document
Image Dataset with 162 different document contents (SRDID162)
consisting of 162 genuine document images, as well as 2592 low
and high-quality recaptured document images, respectively. Our
experimental protocol involves training with high-quality ID im-
ages and testing with SRDID162 dataset of diverse contents and
image qualities. Compared to a SOTA data augmentation ap-
proach for recaptured natural images, our FMAG & M 2 FM
approach achieves a signiﬁcant improvement of 49.15% or 22.50
percentage points in average EER on the generic deep learning
backbones.
Index Terms—Document presentation attack detection, domain
generalization, moiré pattern.

because existing approaches [19], [20], [21] rely on the moiré

available in the blurred samples with degraded high-frequency

In this paper, we employ the model of screen-recaptured dis-
tortion to address the limitations of existing DPAD approaches.
First, the distortion model facilitates the augmentation of the
moiré pattern feature in the frequency domain and general-

document contents. Speciﬁcally, the distortion model of the
screen-recaptured channel is studied and employed in devising
the locations of different moiré spectral peaks (MSPs) with prior

knowledge of MSPs, we further propose the frequency-domain
moiré augmentation (FMAG) strategy that enhances the region

mask moiré frequency modeling (M2 FM) scheme that recovers

distortion model and a pre-trained DPAD classiﬁer based on

II.-B. DPAD Methods Under Screen-Recaptured Channel
There are some traditional methods based on handcrafted
features for presentation attack detection of natural images,
such as recaptured detection based on Edge Proﬁles Learning
Dictionaries (EPLD) [26], Image Statistics (IS) [27], and Local
Tenary Count of Prediction Error (LTC-PE) [28]. These methods
performed well on the datasets of recaptured natural images,
e.g., NTU-ROSE [29] and ICL-COMMSP [26]. However, as
demonstrated in Section IV-B, the performance of these meth-
ods is unsatisfactory under document images across different
contents.
Recently, Chen et al. [17] proposed a general DPAD scheme
for different document samples, including those recaptured from
displays. It is achieved by inspecting the forensic similarity
between the questioned document image and a genuine refer-
ence. Polevoy et al. [18] shared a video dataset on recaptured
IDs (DLC2021). The results of deep learning-based backbones
are provided as baselines for future research. However, the
cross-domain experimental results in [17] suggest poor gen-
eralization performance under document images with different
contents, e.g., IDs, contracts, and receipts. Many existing DPAD
methods only consider limited types of document contents [13],
[30], [31], [32]. Without ﬁnetuning by samples from the target
domain, the generalization performance of these approaches is
not satisfactory.
To improve the generalization performance across different
content, Benalcazar et al. [19] developed a synthetic document
image generation scheme by combining moiré patterns extracted
from images with uniform backgrounds and genuine document
images. The synthetic document images improve the amount
and the diversity of training data in the DPAD network. A
similar moiré pattern synthesis approach has been employed by
Yang et al. [20] for improving the performance of presentation
attack detection for natural images. Markham et al. [21] used
GAN-based generation method to learn pixel grid texture, spatial
aliasing, and color distortions present in recaptured samples and
subsequently transferred these characteristics to genuine images.
Chen et al. [25] proposed the CMA method, which utilizes color
artifacts as a novel forensic feature to enhance the detection per-
formance of screen-recaptured document images across black-
and-white ofﬁce documents. However, as demonstrated by our
experiment in Section IV-C, existing DPAD methods [19], [21],
[25] show less satisfactory on low-quality document images.
This is because many low-quality samples are subject to blurring
distortion, which is essentially a low-pass ﬁlter. As shown in
Fig. 1(b), the moiré patterns in high-frequency bands are lost in
the low-quality samples with weak forensic traces.

III.-C. Masked Moiré Frequency Modeling (M 2 FM)
In Section III-B, we improve the generalization performance
of the DPAD schemes by employing the FMAG strategy which
augments the spectral characteristic of moiré patterns in the
recaptured document images. However, the blurring distortion
diminishes the MSPs in low-quality samples, which degrades
the performance of DPAD schemes trained by the FMAG strat-
egy. This is an important issue since a practical DPAD system
processes samples with different qualities.
To deal with the low-quality blurry samples, traditional meth-
ods may pre-ﬁlter the input samples according to different image
qualities and deal with the low-quality ones separately. However,
such a strategy results in an ensemble classiﬁer of different
DPAD methods which is of high computational complexity.
Thus, we aim to build an efﬁcient DPAD framework based
on backbones trained by our FMAG strategy regardless of the
sample quality.
Speciﬁcally, we propose to ﬁrst recover the MSPs in the
low-quality blurry recaptured samples before performing the
DPAD task. In this part, we reformulate the MSPs recovery as a
spectral reconstruction task. A low-quality blurry sample, with
low amplitude in the high-frequency spectrum, is considered a
low-passed version of the high-quality sample. Then, the MSPs
recovery process employs the low-quality samples in estimating
the high-frequency spectrum in the high-quality counterpart.
Inspired by the Masked Frequency Modeling (MFM) [42]
that applies a mask to speciﬁc frequency components (e.g.,
high-frequency/low-frequency information) of an image and
then uses the unmasked frequency component to reconstruct
or predict the missing frequencies, we estimate the loss in high-
frequency components caused by blurring degradation through
a frequency masking operation. To address this, we propose the
masked moiré frequency modeling (M 2 FM) scheme to train
an efﬁcient backbone for the spectrum reconstruction task by an
accurate masking strategy. Both MFM and our M 2 FM are based
on the concept of the famous Masked Image Modeling (MIM)
technique [43], [44], where certain regions of the input image
are deliberately hidden (masked), and the model is tasked with
predicting or reconstructing the missing parts.

IV . DATABASE AND EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
In this section, we introduce our recaptured document image
dataset and other related datasets from previous works. We then
compare the generalization performance of our method with
other baseline and state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods.
IV .-A. The Datasets and Experiment Protocols
IV-A1) Our SRDID162: We have collected a large-scale
and comprehensive document image dataset, named Screen
Recaptured Document Image Dataset with 162 templates (SR-
DID162), which includes 5346 samples of both genuine and
recaptured images. The database collection procedure consists
of three steps. First, we retrieve and gather genuine document
images with several authoritative search engines. These images
are obtained by imaging the original hard copies. Next, we
use image editing software such as Photoshop and GNU Im-
age Manipulation Program (GIMP) [45] to remove and tamper
with some key information, such as name, address, and grades.
Finally, we collect tampered document images by shooting the
monitor using devices with different hardware speciﬁcations
to obtain diverse recaptured document images. As shown in
Fig. 1, the tampering operation alters some critical texts, but the
recapturing process conceals the forensic traces of the tampered
area, such as differences in color and edge sharpness. In the
following, we will present some practical considerations for
collecting these document images.
Retrieving Genuine Document Images: To select source im-
ages for our dataset, we adopt the same genuine document im-
ages in [14]. As shown in Fig. 4(a) and (b), the documents include
various types of certiﬁcates, transcripts, and licenses in Chinese
and English. We preferred samples with high resolution (higher
than 1500 × 1500 pixels) to allow realistic document image
editing. These genuine images were retrieved from different
online sources. Thus, they have undergone unknown distortions
(such as compression, scaling, and blurring during or before
uploading).

account for the samples with weak forensic features. To evaluate

consider experimental Protocol 1 in this section employing DM
as the training set and SwMP and DLC2021 as the testing sets.
In our training set, the positive (attack) class has signiﬁcantly
more images than the negative class. To mitigate this issue, we
adopt the data-level oversampling strategy [47] that duplicates
the negative class and keep each mini-batch balanced.
In this protocol, we ﬁrst applied the traditional methods based

In this part, we demonstrate the advantage of incorporating
M2 FM to reconstruct the missing spectral features in the low-
quality samples before performing DPAD. During the training
phase, we employ three types of samples, i.e., genuine images in
DM, recaptured images in DM, and recaptured images without
moiré in D1. According to the LF in M2 FM, the reconstructed
spectrum of recaptured images should contain high-frequency
characteristics corresponding to moiré patterns. Therefore, the
genuine and recaptured images (with moiré pattern) in DM
can be directly used as input for training M 2 FM. However,
for the recaptured images without moiré pattern, we apply the

encoder and decoder while freezing the DPAD network D,
which has been pre-trained by the FMAG method. Speciﬁcally,
taking ViT as an example, the encoder comprises 12 Transformer
layers. The input image is initially divided into 16 ×16 patches
through a patch embedding layer and processed by the encoder
to extracts forensic feature with feature dimensions of 768.
These features are subsequently fed into the decoder for image
reconstruction. The decoder consists of a lightweight linear
layer and a pixel-shufﬂe [58] layer. Moreover, we set parameter
τ =6 4based on prior knowledge of device parameters and the
MSPL algorithm in Section III-A, which is different from the
conﬁgurations in [42]. This choice ensures the inclusion of all
MSPs in the ML(u). To emphasize the restoration of high-
frequency features, we set the parameters λ1 =2 and λ2 =1 .
Additionally, considering the importance of classiﬁcation loss,
which is more signiﬁcant than the visual ﬁdelity of outputs from
M2 FM network, we experimentally set the parameters α1 =0 .2
and α2 =1 , respectively.

As shown in Table VIII, it is obvious that the proposed
F M A G&M 2 FM method is the strategy that leads to the best
performance. In the following, we present the insights from the
experimental results under different ablation conﬁgurations.
First, it is important to understand the limitations of tradi-
tional data augmentation strategies. The CONVL strategy does
not consider the characteristic of distortions in the document
recapturing process. On the one hand, a practical document
authentication scenario does not cover augmentation procedures
like rotation or cropping. Users must capture the image directly
in a predetermined frame area and in the correct direction. On
the other hand, the contrast and sharpness adjustment may atten-
uate the forensic features, leading to performance degradation.
Among them, contrast adjustment merely redistributes the pixel
values by mapping the darkest and brightest pixel values to
the minimum and maximum values. However, in images with
relatively weak moiré texture, moiré features will become even
less discernible. Sharpness adjustment, achieved through a ﬁxed
ﬁlter, enhances high-frequency components. Nevertheless, it not
only neglects that the MSPs discussed in Section III-A1 are
location-speciﬁc for each recaptured image but may also weaken
the MSPs discussed in Section III-A2 and III-A3. For instance,
the EER of ViT-B16 increased by 0.53 p.p. under the CONVL
strategy.
Second, we discuss the limitation of directly applying a high-
pass ﬁlter (HPF). Experimental results demonstrate that the HPF
samples degrade the DPAD performance for the ViT-B16 model
and Swin-B model compared to the FMAG method (the EERs
are increased from 38.70% to 40.15%, from 41.33% to 43.21%,
separately). This is because the high-frequency components not
only include features related to recapturing attack but also cover
other high-frequency details of the image, e.g., sharp edge,
which are irrelevant to the DPAD task.
Third, we discuss the importance of the MSPL strategy in the
spectral domain. The FMAG w/o MSPL method performs data
augmentation in the whole amplitude spectrum. There are slight
performance improvements for the ViT-B16 model and Swin-B
model (the EERs are reduced from 40.74% to 39.64%, from
45.70% to 42.60%, separately) over the CONVL augmentation
method. However, the performances of FMAG w/o MSPL are
not as good as the FMAG strategy. This is because FMAG
w/o MSPL does not exploit the distribution characteristics of
the moiré distortion in the spectral domain. It indicates that
the MSPs of recaptured document images, determined by the
parameters of acquisition devices, contain vital information
for domain generalization. The FMAG method achieves bet-
ter performances than FMAG w/o MSPL across two different
backbones, supporting the importance of our MSPL step.
Last but not least, we discuss the importance of classiﬁer
guidance in the proposed FMAG & M 2 FM method. We can
observe that, without using the classiﬁer loss, the FMAG &
M2 FM w/o LD method shows an average EER degradation
of 11.19 p.p. compared to the proposed FMAG & M 2 FM. This
degradation arises from the fact that our method takes advantage
of the supervision from the DPAD classiﬁcation result, while the
FMAG & M 2 FM w/o LD method is self-supervised without
labeled information. It is noted that our objective is not model
pre-training but rather the spectral reconstruction process for the
DPAD task. Therefore, the proposed FMAG & M 2 FM employs
the DPAD task labels as additional information to supervise the
reconstruction model and achieve better generalization perfor-
mance towards samples with weak forensic traces.
V. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we propose a DPAD method, named FMAG
&M 2 FM of good generalization performance on document
images with different contents and qualities. The proposed
method is compared with some deep learning-based backbones
and the existing DPAD methods, including CycleGAN [21],
TMTP [19], and CMA [25] under challenging cross-domain
experimental protocols. Experimental results show that the ex-
isting DPAD methods fail to generalize due to the diversity of
document sources, countries, and variations in capture condi-
tions. Meanwhile, the proposed method improves the generic
deep learning backbones with a reduction of 5.35 and 34.39 p.p.
