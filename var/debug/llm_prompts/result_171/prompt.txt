Abstract—In the realm of the Internet of things (IoT),
radio frequency identification (RFID) technology is
essential for linking physical objects to digital data.
The introduction of harmonic technology has expanded
RFID’s applications by improving sensitivity and enabling
communication with wireless fidelity (Wi-Fi) networks.
However, this trend faces challenges, notably interference
from strong Wi-Fi signals, which impacts RFID-based
sensing systems. This paper proposes the WiFID (Wi-Fi
and RFID) algorithm, which enables Wi-Fi devices to
sense weak RFID harmonic signals. Adaptive subcarrier
deallocation minimally reduces Wi-Fi throughput while
effectively limiting interference with RFID harmonic
sensing. Experimental validation on the universal soft-
ware radio peripheral (USRP) platform demonstrates
that WiFID successfully detects 90% of RFID harmonics
at −30 dBm, with only a minimal 4% decrease in Wi-Fi
throughput.
Keywords—RFID harmonic, wireless sensing, Wi-Fi, in-

II. RFID H ARMONIC PRELIMINARY
Passive RFID tags operate without an internal power
source, relying instead on energy harvested from the signals
emitted by the reader to function effectively. A typical pas-
sive ultrahigh frequency (UHF) RFID tags comprises three
main components: a dipole antenna, a multipoint control unit
(MCU), and a power management unit. The power manage-
ment unit mainly consists of a rectifier circuit that includes
several nonlinear components, such as diodes and capaci-
tors. This setup gives the RFID tag a nonlinear behavior dur-
ing communication[12-13]. As a result, the tag does not only
backscatter the signal at its fundamental frequency, but also
generates harmonics, which are integer multiples of the fun-
damental frequency components[14].

In this section, we will detail the challenges encountered
during the implementation of WiFID and how we addressed

A. Detect RFID Harmonics Under Wi-Fi Interference
Via Delay-Correlation and Matched Filtering
To ensure that Wi-Fi does not have an impact on RFID
harmonics sensing, we need to detect the presence of RFID
harmonics in the Wi-Fi communication’s channel firstly. Due
to the nonlinear attenuation, RFID harmonic signals are usu-
ally weaker than Wi-Fi signals, and are often overwhelmed
by Wi-Fi (as shown in Fig. 3(a)). Consequently, the primary
challenge for the WiFID system is to detect RFID harmonic
signals that are masked by stronger Wi-Fi signals. A common
technique employed is matched filtering; however, as depicted
in Fig. 3(b), it is evident that even with higher intensity RFID
reader signals, discerning significant peaks post-matched fil-
tering remains challenging.
To solve this problem, WiFID designs a delay-
correlation[15] based harmonic signal detection method
to amplify RFID harmonic signals obscured by Wi-Fi signals.
The basic idea of the method is that, in order to reduce
nonlinear distortion and improve the performance of the
communication system, Wi-Fi adopts various techniques
to suppress the peak-to-average ratio (PAPR) of its signal,

B. Ascertain the harmonic signal originate from RFID
tag via bandpass filtering and matched filtering
Although the delayed cross-correlation algorithm em-
ployed in section A successfully detected RFID harmonics
hidden within the Wi-Fi signals, as illustrated in Fig. 4(b),
only the more intense harmonic signals emitted by readers
have been detectable. To that end, the second challenge lies in
the fact that the tag harmonics, which carry essential sensing
information, reflect signals with exceedingly low power. Even
after amplification, these signals remain difficult to detect ac-
curately.
WiFID capitalizes on the feature that both reader and tag
signals in RFID communication operate at the same fre-
quency. This implies that if WiFID detects harmonic signals
from the reader, any tag harmonic signals must also be present
within the same frequency band. Initially, WiFID performs a
spectral analysis on the received signals to gather the spectrum
of the RFID harmonics, as depicted in Fig. 5(a). Utilizing

C. Ensure Both Sensing and Communication Perfor-
mance Via Mode Selection and Subcarrier Nullifica-
tion
After confirming the presence of RFID tag harmonic sig-
nals in the environment, it can be determined that a RFID har-
monic sensing system is operational. Clearly, rather than re-
stricting Wi-Fi communications to ensure the performance of
the RFID harmonic sensing system, a more rational approach
involves adaptively nulling Wi-Fi subcarriers to avoid the fre-
quency bands occupied by RFID harmonics. According to the
Gen-2 protocol, the channel bandwidth of RFID is generally
500 kHz[18], while the Wi-Fi channel occupies a bandwidth
of 20 MHz and is divided into 64 subcarriers. Wi-Fi uses 52
subcarriers for data transmission, and 4 out of these subcarrier
are allocated to pilot signal (nunmbered in −21, −7, 7, and
21) to robustly guard the Wi-Fi networks against frequency
offsets and phase noises [16-17]. Thus, there are two potential
frequency domain overlap modes between RFID and Wi-Fi:
(1) RFID harmonics occupy the frequency bands of pilot sub-
carriers, or (2) RFID harmonics do not occupy the pilot sub-
carrier bands.
WiFID has designed subcarrier allocation schemes for
these two modes and selects appropriate subcarrier nulling
schemes based on the spectral information of the RFID har-
monics. Specifically, when there are no RFID harmonics,
Wi-Fi will communicate using the normal subcarrier layout
(as shown in Fig. 6). When the frequency band occupied by
the RFID harmonics does not overlap with the pilot subcar-
riers, WiFID will simply nullify the subcarriers that overlap

IV. S YSTEM DESIGN
Fig. 7 shows the architecture of WiFID, which involves
three main module: harmonic detection module, tag harmonic
confirmation module, and Wi-Fi modification module. In this
section, we will briefly introduce how WiFID works.
Harmonic detection module. Upon receiving a signal, the
module first applies a delay correlation technique aimed at
enhancing the quality of signals with significant fluctuations
between consecutive time slots. This is achieved by means
of a temporal correlation analysis that identifies and enhances
signals with strong temporal characteristics. After initial en-
hancement, the amplified signal is subjected to matched fil-
tering. It aligns the input signal with a pre-defined template
to maximize the correlation and thus effectively detect RFID
harmonic signals. The combination of delayed correlation and
matched filtering ensures that the system can effectively detect
weak RFID harmonic signals.
Tag harmonic confirmation module. After initial detec-
tion, the module extracts spectral information from signals
that have been identified as having RFID harmonics. By ac-
curately analyzing this spectrum information, the bandpass
filter is adaptively designed to eliminate out-of-band Wi-Fi
interference, otherwise the Wi-Fi interference will mask the
RFID harmonic tag signal and affect the subsequent process-
ing. After filtering, the system will use matched filtering tech-
niques to rigorously detect the presence of the desired RFID
harmonic tag signal to ensure that only accurate signals are

V. P ERFORMANCE EVALUATION
In this section, we conduct comprehensive experiments to
evaluate the performance of WiFID, and present a case study
to demonstrate the practical effectiveness of WiFID in RFID

A. Experimental Setup
We conducted detailed WiFID performance tests on the
USRP platform in a 11 ×6 meter lab. Four USRP N210 de-
vices were deployed as shown in Fig. 8: one as an RFID reader
on the 812 MHz band, another as a sniffer at 2.436 GHz to
monitor RFID tag harmonics, and two for Wi-Fi communi-
cation at 2.432 GHz. The antenna of the RFID reader was

B. Overall Performance
In this experiment, we varied the RFID reader’s transmis-
sion power to achieve different signal-to-noise ratio (SNR),
keeping all device positions fixed. RFID harmonic power was
set between −10 dBm and −50 dBm in 10 dBm steps, while
Wi-Fi power was fixed at 15 dBm. The WiFID AP detected
RFID and tag harmonics, and results were compared with sig-
nal copies recorded by the RFID reader. To ensure data reli-
ability, we conducted 100 repeated experiments for each set-
ting.
1) Performance of Harmonic Signal Detection: We first
evaluated the performance of WiFID in detecting RFID and
EPC harmonic signals. The result is shown in Fig. 9(a), when
the power of RFID harmonics was above −30 dBm, WiFID’s
detection of RFID harmonics and electronic product code
(EPC) harmonics is maintained at a high level of accuracy,
above 98% and above 90%, respectively. As RFID harmonic
power decreased, WiFID’s detection accuracy gradually de-
clined. At −50 dBm, detection accuracy dropped sharply to

cline occurs because low RFID harmonic power limits the ef-
fectiveness of WiFID’s delay-correlation method in amplify-
ing RFID harmonic signals, thereby affecting the subsequent
detection of EPC harmonics and Wi-Fi processing.

eliminated is limited, they have limited interference with Wi-
Fi communications, which means when using WiFID, Wi-Fi
throughput is only slightly lower and not affecting Wi-Fi us-
age.

6) Impact of Tag Number: We also investigated the impact
of tag quantity on system performance. Using H47 tags and
keeping other parameters constant, we tested the system with
1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 tags sequentially.
Fig. 14 shows that when fewer than three tags are used, sys-

tags increases, RFID harmonic detection accuracy declines.
This is mainly due to the ALOHA-like channel access pro-
tocol in the RFID system, where more tags increase channel

D. Case Study
Vibration detection is a crucial application of RFID har-
monic sensing, playing a significant role in monitoring the
operational status of machinery and diagnosing faults. In
this section, we demonstrate the effectiveness of WiFID in
enhancing sensing robustness through a practical RFID har-
monic vibration detection system. TagVibra, a study pub-
lished by Li et al. in 2023 [10], achieves accurate detection
of sub-millimeter vibrations by leveraging RFID harmonics
combined with differential phase amplification techniques.
We implemented TagVibra and set its operating frequency to
overlap with Wi-Fi, testing it on mechanical vibrations with
amplitudes ranging from 0.3 mm to 2.3 mm at a frequency of
100 Hz. We then observed the performance of the TagVibra
system before and after the application of WiFID.
Fig. 15 shows that without WiFID, Wi-Fi interference
caused significant detection errors in TagVibra, reaching up
to 50 Hz. However, with WiFID, the RFID harmonic fre-
quency band was cleared, allowing TagVibra to function nor-
mally with a reduced measurement error of 1.3 Hz. This high-
lights WiFID’s ability to detect RFID harmonics and adjust
Wi-Fi subcarriers to maintain RFID sensing effectiveness.
VI. C ONCLUSION
This paper introduces WiFID, a novel approach designed to
facilitate the coexistence of Wi-Fi and RFID harmonics within
the same spectral environment. WiFID optimizes spectrum
utilization and minimizes interference by enhancing the de-
tection and differentiation of RFID harmonic signals in Wi-Fi
bands. Extensive testing on the USRP platform has demon-
strated that WiFID can accurately detect RFID harmonic com-
munications with up to 90% accuracy, significantly enhancing
the performance of RFID systems without compromising Wi-
Fi throughput.
