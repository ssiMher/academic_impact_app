Eavesdropping on human voice is one of the most common but harmful threats to personal privacy. Glasses are in direct
contact with human face, which could sense facial motions when users speak, so human speech contents could be inferred by
sensing the movements of glasses. In this paper, we present a live voice eavesdropping method,RF-Mic, which utilizes common
glasses attached with a low-cost RFID tag to sense subtle facial speech dynamics for inferring possible voice contents. When a
user with a glasses, which is attached an RFID tag on the glass bridge, is speaking, RF-Mic first collects RF signals through
forward propagation and backscattering. Then, body motion interference is eliminated from the collected RF signals through
a proposed Conditional Denoising AutoEncoder (CDAE) network. Next, RF-Mic extracts three kinds of facial speech dynamic
features (i.e., facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations) by designing three different deep-learning
models. Based on the extracted features, a facial speech dynamics model is constructed for live voice eavesdropping. Extensive
experiments in different real environments demonstrate that RF-Mic can achieve robust and accurate human live voice
eavesdropping.

1 INTRODUCTION
Voice eavesdropping has become one of the most concerning security issues in recent years, as voice commu-
nication between users is an unencrypted transmission channel, making it easy to obtain personal sensitive
information. Nowadays, people often wear glasses in daily life due to various reasons, such as myopia, aesthetics,
and sunshade requirement. Glasses are in direct contact with human face, which could sense facial motions (i.e.,
facial speech dynamics) when users speak. Since human facial speech dynamics and voice contents are closely
related [25], it is possible to eavesdrop on the voice by sensing facial speech dynamics leveraging glasses worn
by users. Recently, some researchers explore utilizing customized glasses equipped with various sensors to sense
facial speech dynamics for human voice eavesdropping. For example, Face-Mic[30] leverages motion sensors on
AR/VR headsets to sense subtle facial dynamics for inferring human speech contents. V-Speech [21] captures
voice signals with a vibration sensor located in the nasal pads of smart glasses for speech eavesdropping. However,
these methods employ either VR headsets that are only used in certain situations, or specially customized glasses
that equipped with motion sensor, which leads to a high cost and limits their application scenarios greatly.
Recently, Radio-frequency identification (RFID) technology has been widely exploited from communication to
lots of sensing tasks, such as localization[40], activity recognition[9], and vital signs monitoring[33], etc. RFID
tag has the advantages of small size, deploying crypticity, and low cost, so it is suitable to be attached to various
common glasses for sensing human facial motions, which provides the possibility of live voice eavesdropping
leveraging RF signals. To achieve live voice eavesdropping with an RFID tag attached on glasses, we face several
challenges in practice. First, we should accurately capture the subtle facial dynamics of users leveraging RF signals
when users speak to achieve accurate voice eavesdropping. Second, we should effectively extract comprehensive
facial speech dynamics features including facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, etc., from RF signals to
construct an adaptable speech eavesdropping model. Third, we should remove various interference, such as
human body motion interference from collected RF signals and user specificity from extracted features to achieve
robust live voice eavesdropping.
In this paper, we first explore the feasibility of live voice eavesdropping leveraging an RFID tag attached on
glasses, and find that RF signals can capture subtle facial speech dynamics including facial movements, bone-
borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations. Motivated by the observations, we propose a live voice eavesdropping
system, RF-Mic, which utilizes an RFID tag on glasses to achieve human voice eavesdropping. Specifically, in
RF-Mic, the signal transmitting antenna first sends electromagnetic signals to activate the RFID tag attached
on the glasses bridge, and then the activated tag reflects RF signals to the signal receiving antenna. When a
user who wears the glasses is speaking, RF-Mic collects RF signals reflected from the RFID tag to sense the
user’s subtle facial speech dynamics. RF-Mic first pre-processes the collected RF signals to eliminate body motion
interference for facial speech dynamics sensing through a Conditional Denoising AutoEncoder (CDAE) network.
According to the pre-processed RF signals,RF-Mic extracts facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne
vibration features by designing Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)-based model, Convolutional Recurrent Neural
Network (CRNN)-based model, and Deep Residual Shrinkage Network (DRSN)-based model, respectively, and
then fuses the three kinds of extracted features based on a self-attention method. Further, RF-Mic removes the
user specificity in the fused facial speech dynamics features by utilizing a contrast learning network to obtain
user-irrelevant but voice content-related facial speech dynamics features for voice eavesdropping. Finally, based
on the user-irrelevant features, a facial speech dynamics model is constructed for live voice eavesdropping. We
validate the performance of RF-Mic by conducting extensive experiments in real-world environments. The results
demonstrate that RF-Mic can effectively eavesdrop on live voice in different scenarios.
We highlight our main contributions as follows:
•We propose a RF-Mic system, which can sense facial speech dynamics for voice eavesdropping through
common glasses attached with an RFID tag.

2.3 Capturing Different Facial Speech Dynamics via RF Signals
Human speech contents are encoded by a set of facial speech dynamics, such as facial movements, bone-borne
vibrations, and airborne vibrations, etc. [8, 25, 30]. We first utilize RF signals to sense these facial speech dynamics
and then study the influence of different speech contents on these facial speech dynamics.
We attach an RFID tag on glasses A and B and invite a user with the glasses to speak single-syllable words
(i.e., "one", "two", and "three") and multi-syllabic words (i.e., "hello" and "excellent") without pronouncing, so
only facial movements are sensed by RF signals. The corresponding spectrograms of RF signals for glasses A
and B are shown in Fig. 4 (b). From the figure, we can observe that the spectrograms corresponding to facial
movements are distributed at low frequencies. Hence, human facial movements can bring low-frequency signal
responses when users speak. Furthermore, to study the influence of airborne vibrations, we replay the speech (i.e.,
"one", "two", "three", "hello", and "excellent") with a smartphone towards the glasses on the user’s face. As shown
in Fig. 4(c), RF signals at high-frequency (i.e., > 100𝐻𝑧) can be observed. Hence, airborne vibrations can bring
high-frequency signal responses when users speak. Since RF signals corresponding to bone-borne vibrations can
not be measured directly, we remove the corresponding parts of Fig. 4 (b) and 4 (c) from Fig. 4 (a) and obtain
the spectrograms of RF signals corresponding to bone-borne vibrations. As shown in Fig. 4 (d), the bone-borne
vibrations mainly impact spectrograms of RF signals at high-frequency. Hence, bone-borne vibrations can bring
high-frequency signal responses when users speak. Moreover, the influence of the three kinds of facial speech
dynamics (i.e., facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations) are various for different speech
contents. According to Fig. 4, we further calculate the proportion of three facial speech dynamics, the results
show that when the user speak word "one", the proportion of facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and
airborne vibrations are 0.33, 0.56, and 0.11, respectively. When the user speaks word "three", the proportion of
facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations are 0.48, 0.37, and 0.15, respectively. Hence, we
consider employing the self-attention mechanism[32] for feature fusion, which can fuse features of the three
kinds of facial speech dynamics with adaptive weights for different speech contents.

3 SYSTEM OVERVIEW
To infer possible speech contents leveraging RF signals, we propose a live voice eavesdropping system, RF-Mic,
which utilizes common glasses attached with an RFID tag to sense subtle facial speech dynamics for live voice
eavesdropping. In this section, we first introduce the attack model and eavesdropping scenario, and then we
present the system overview of RF-Mic.

3.1 Attack Model and Eavesdropping Scenario
3.1.1 Attack Model. Human speech contents are encoded by a set of facial speech dynamics, such as facial
movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations[8, 25, 30]. Since glasses are in direct contact with
users’ faces, the facial speech dynamics could drive subtle glasses vibrations when users speak, which offers a
possibility of capturing the facial speech dynamics through the vibrations of the glasses. In a practical deployment
scenario, an attacker secretly attaches a small RFID tag on a victim’s glasses bridge, and the victim cannot realize
the existence of the RFID tag on his/her glasses. By capturing the human facial speech dynamics leveraging the
RFID tag on glasses, the attacker can launch human live voice eavesdropping without the victim’s permission.
With the rapid development of RFID technology, the size of RFID tags is becoming smaller and smaller. Nowadays,
the size of the smallest RFID tag is only 2 ×3 ×0.55𝑚𝑚and the cost of such an RFID tag is not high[ 27], so
attackers can attach such a small RFID tag on victims’ glasses that victims cannot notice it to achieve live voice
eavesdropping. Through the RFID-based live voice eavesdropping, attackers can infer key speech contents (e.g.,
digits, hot-words, and phrases) when victims speak. These speech contents may contain some private sensitive
information, such as telephone numbers, credit card passwords, and social security numbers, etc. Hence, attackers
could eavesdrop on victims’ private sensitive information from the speaking contents. Exposing such private
sensitive information could result in identity theft, credit card fraud, and confidential information leakage, etc.,
which puts users’ security and privacy at high risk.
3.1.2 Eavesdropping Scenario. In a practical deployment scenario, attackers can launch live voice eavesdropping
attacks without victims’ permission when victims with glasses attached on an RFID tag speak at home or in a
meeting room/office. Assuming that an attacker secretly attaches a small RFID tag on a victim’s glasses bridge,
and the RFID antenna is camouflaged into decorations and placed on the meeting table/bookshelf in the meeting
room/office. When the victim with the glasses is speaking, the victim’s facial speech dynamics drive the glasses
vibrations, so the RFID tag on the glasses could sense the victim’s facial speech dynamics. The attacker leverages
the camouflaged RFID antenna to collect RF signals continuously when the victim speaks. By analyzing the
spectrogram energy distribution of RF signals, the attacker can infer possible speech contents from RF signals.

the activated tag feedbacks RF signals through back-scattering, and the signals are received by signal receiving

4 SIGNALS PRE-PROCESSING
In RF-Mic, RF signal transmitting antenna first sends electromagnetic signals to activate the RFID tag on the
glasses. Then, the activated tag feedbacks RF signals through back-scattering, and the signals are received by
the signal receiving antenna. The received RF signals need to be pre-processed before extracting facial speech
dynamics features. In this section, we first segment the received RF signals into episodes that represent each
speaking word, and then body motion interference is removed from the segmented RF signals.

words "how" "are" and "you". It can be observed from the figure that the time intervals between two successive
words are significant. To segment the RF signals, we propose an energy-based sliding window signal segmentation
method based on the fact that fluctuation of RF signals becomes larger when a user speaks. The energy of RF

4.2 Body Motion Interference Removal
In addition to facial speech dynamics, human body motions can also be sensed by RF signals, which affects the
sensing of facial speech dynamics. To achieve accurate live voice eavesdropping, the body motion interference
need to be removed from the received RF signals.
To remove body motion interference in RF signals, we design a Conditional Denoising AutoEncoder (CDAE)
based on signal generate technique[16] to generate spectrograms of RF signals without body motion interference
from the collected RF signals. The CDAE can remove the impact of various body motions, which contains
both small body motions (such as nodding/shaking head) and large body motions (such as walking/gesturing).
As shown in Figure. 7, we collect RF signals’ spectrograms containing signal response for facial movements,
bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations as ground truth when users speak (which do not involve body
motions), and record the corresponding label 𝑦, i.e., the name of each speaking word. RF signals’ spectrograms
with body motions are collected as the input 𝑥 of CDAE, which contains signal response for body motions, facial
movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations. In the CDAE, the spectrograms with body motions
(i.e., input 𝑥, which contains signals response for body motions, facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and
airborne vibrations) are processed by the encoder and decoder to generate a new spectrogram (i.e., output ˆ𝑥,
which contains signals response for facial movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations). The

5 FEATURE EXTRACTION
Based on the pre-processed RF signals, RF-Mic further extracts features of facial speech dynamics, i.e, facial
movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations, respectively. In this section, we first introduce feature
extraction of different facial speech dynamics based on their own signal characteristics, and then an adaptive
weights method is introduced for feature fusion.
5.1 Facial Movements Feature Extraction
The received RF signals correspond to three kinds of facial speech dynamics, i.e, facial movements, bone-
borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations. To extract facial movement features, RF-Mic first separates RF signals
corresponding to facial movements from the received signals. As analyzed in Section 2.3, RF signals corresponding
to facial movements are presented at low frequency (i.e., 100𝐻𝑧), so a low-pass filter with a cut-off frequency of
100𝐻𝑧 is utilized to separate and obtain RF signals corresponding to facial movements.
After that, we further extract facial movement features from the separated RF signals corresponding to facial
movements. Human speaking behaviors drive facial movements, which causes facial muscles contract and relax
randomly during speaking. Facial muscles encode both speech characteristics (e.g., phoneme, rhythm, and loud-
ness) and bio-metric characteristics (e.g., speaking behaviors, facial shapes change, and tissue movements)[25].
Since the speech and bio-metric characteristics are time-varying, we consider utilizing RNN models to extract
temporal features corresponding to facial movements. To capture human facial movement features as comprehen-
sively as possible when users speak, we choose 10 most commonly used time-domain statistical characteristics
(i.e., maximum values, minimum values, mean values, variance, range, root mean square, median, quartile,
skewness, and kurtosis) in statistics to describe human facial movement characteristics. From a statistical view,

5.2 Bone-borne Vibration Feature Extraction

separates RF signals corresponding to bone-borne vibrations. Since RF signals corresponding to bone-borne
vibrations and airborne vibration are distributed at similar frequencies, which can not be separated by simple
filters. However, because the two kinds of RF signals come from different signal sources, we utilize a signal source
separation technique[35] to separate RF signals corresponding to bone-borne vibrations and airborne vibrations.
Based on the signal source separation technique, we separate RF signals corresponding to bone-borne vibrations
from the mixed signals of bone-borne vibrations and airborne vibrations.

5.3 Airborne Vibration Feature Extraction
According to signal source separation technique, we separate the RF signals corresponding to airborne vibrations
from the mixed signals of bone-borne vibrations and airborne vibrations. Since the airborne vibrations are usually
affected by wind noise[26], we need to extract airborne vibration features without wind noise.
Deep Residual Shrinkage Network (DRSN)[49] is a feature extraction algorithm that can extract features from
signals with noise, so we consider utilizing DRSN to extract features of airborne vibrations from spectrograms
of RF signals containing wind noise. Fig. 10 shows the architecture of DRSN, which includes two convolution-
pooling units and 3 ResNet blocks. Each convolution-pooling unit is composed of a convolution layer and a
pooling layer. The RF signals’ spectrogram with wind noise is first input to the convolution-pooling units for
learning the relationship between time and frequency. Due to the large size of spectrogram, a max-pooling layer
is attached to each convolution layer for dimension reduction in the convolution-pooling units. The output of
convolution-pooling units is a feature representation of spectrogram with wind noise. After that, the feature
representation is input to three ResNet blocks for wind noise removal and feature extraction. Each ResNet block
contains three convolution layers and a sub-network. The feature representation is first convoluted by three
convolution layers in the ResNet blocks for obtaining a fine-grained feature representation, which is a features
matrix. Then, the fine-grained feature representation is input to a sub-network at two paths, which can learn
an adaptive soft threshold for the spectrogram to remove wind noise. The output of path 1 is the fine-grained
feature representation after global average pooling, and the output of path 2 is a parameter 𝛼 in range of [0,1]

5.4 Feature Fusion Based on Self-Attention
The extracted three kinds of facial speech dynamics features reflect human speaking characteristics of facial
movements, bone-borne vibrations, and airborne vibrations, respectively. To comprehensively describe the
characteristics of human speech, RF-Mic needs to fuse the three kinds of facial speech dynamics features
for constructing voice eavesdropping model. In the process of feature fusion, a straightforward method is to
concatenate the three kinds of facial speech dynamics features for describing human speaking characteristics.
However, the influence of the three kinds of facial speech dynamics features are various for different speech
contents. For example, as shown in Fig. 4 (a) in Section 2.3, when a user speaks "three", the proportion of facial
movements in all facial speech dynamics is larger than that when the user speaks "one", i.e, when a user speaks
"three", the influence of facial movements is larger than the user speaks "one". When the user speaks word "one",
the proportion of bone-borne vibrations in all facial speech dynamics is larger than that when the user speaks
"three", i.e, when a user speaks "one", the influence of bone-borne vibrations is larger than when the user speaks
"three". Hence, we consider employing the self-attention mechanism[32] for feature fusion, which can fuse the
features of three kinds of facial speech dynamics with adaptive weights for different speech contents.

6 MODEL CONSTRUCTION FOR LIVE VOICE EAVESDROPOING
Based on the extracted facial speech dynamics features, we further construct a live voice eavesdropping model to
infer possible speech contents. Since human facial speech dynamics usually contain user specificity, such as speech
behavior, face shapes, and tissue properties, we need to first remove the user specificity in the extracted facial
speech dynamics features. After that, we construct a facial speech dynamics model for live voice eavesdropping
based on the user-irrelevant features.
6.1 User Specificity Removal Based on Contrast Learning

the user specificity in the extracted facial speech dynamics features and obtain features only related to speech
contents. The structure of the contrast learning network is shown in Fig. 12. As shown in the Figure, the contrast
learning network is mainly composed of three components, i.e., encoder, projector, and contrast loss. 𝑥𝑖 is an

for obtaining the corresponding feature representation 𝑧. Based on the feature representation 𝑧, a contrast loss

7.4 Impact of Distance
RF signals attenuate as propagating through a distance, so the distance between RFID antenna and the tag on
glasses has a certain impact on the performance of RF-Mic. To explore the impact of distance on the performance
of RF-Mic, we change the distance between RFID antenna and tag on glasses to conduct experiments for all the
12 volunteers and the results are shown in Fig. 21. From the figure, we can observe that both the accuracies and
WCR decrease as the distance increases. This is because as the distance increases, the signal strength of received
RF signals significantly decreases, which induces a low Signal-Noise-Ratio (SNR) and reduces the resolution of
extracted features for voice eavesdropping. However, the performance of RF-Mic dose not decline significantly
in the distance range of 0 ∼1.8𝑚. Specifically, when the distance is 0.2𝑚, the digit eavesdropping accuracy,
hot-word eavesdropping accuracy, and phrase eavesdropping WCR ofRF-Mic are 92.75%, 90.86%, and 91.95%,
respectively. When the distance is within 1.8𝑚, both the single-word (i.e., digit and hot-word) eavesdropping
accuracy and phrase eavesdropping WCR are higher than 85%. Such a distance range is applicable for most voice
eavesdropping scenarios, such as the meeting room/office. Usually, the distance from the meeting table to the
seats in the meeting room and the distance from the desk to the bookshelf in the office are within the range.
Hence, eavesdroppers can employ RF-Mic to achieve natural human live voice eavesdropping within such a
distance range.

the orientation is positive when users are on the right side of 𝑂𝐴, and vice versa. In this experiment, we fix the
distance between RFID antenna and tag on glasses as 1𝑚for variable control, and change the orientation from

WCR of RF-Mic for all the 12 volunteers at different orientations. We can observe from the figure that both

reduced, resulting in voice dynamic features resolution decrease. But when the orientation is within the range of
[−45◦,45◦], both the single-word eavesdropping accuracy and phrase eavesdropping WCR are above 85%. Such a
orientation range is applicable for most voice eavesdropping scenarios, such as the meeting room/office.

7.6 Impact of Voice Volume
Usually, the voice volume of human normal conversation is 40 ∼60 decibels (dB) and it is like whispering when
the sound is below 30 decibels. A higher human voice volume could bring a larger magnitude of facial speech
dynamics, so the volume of a person’s voice may affect the performance ofRF-Mic. We measure the speech volume
using a smartphone at a distance of 0.5𝑚from the volunteers’ mouths. To explore the impact of voice volume for
RF-Mic, we collect RF signals correspond to human speaking and divide the RF signals into groups according to
the volunteer’s speaking voice volume from 40 to 60𝑑𝐵 with 5𝑑𝐵 steps. Fig. 24 shows the single-word and phrase
eavesdropping performance of RF-Mic for different voice volumes. It can be seen that a higher voice volume leads
to a better voice eavesdropping performance. When human voice volume is 60 ∼65𝑑𝐵, RF-Mic achieves a digit
eavesdropping accuracy of 92.54%, hot-word eavesdropping accuracy of 91.36%, and phrase eavesdropping WCR
of 91.75%. Moreover, when human voice volume is large than 40𝑑𝐵, both single-word eavesdropping accuracy
and phrase eavesdropping WCR are larger than 85%. These results can be attributed to that RF-Mic can fuse facial
movement, bone-borne vibration, and airborne vibration features with adaptive weights.

8 RELATED WORK
In this section, we review existing works about RFID-based sensing and voice eavesdropping.
RFID-based sensing.Due to the advantages of low cost and deploy flexibility, RFID tag have been exploited
in lots of sensing applications, such as localization[40], activity recognition[7, 9], and vital signs monitoring[33],
etc. Meanwhile, because of the excellent sensing ability of RF signals, recent years have witnessed the rapid
development of RFID-based vibration detection[19, 41, 45, 46]. For example, TagSound[19] utilizes the harmonic
backscattering of an RFID tag to recover high-frequency and tiny mechanical vibrations. Further, RF-Ear[46]
proposes a contactless vibration sensing system using COTS RFID hardware, which can monitor mechanical
vibrations of multiple devices using a single tag. Recently, RF-DJ[10] leverages a RFID tag to sense the vibrations
of ukulele surface for single tone music recognition.
Remote voice eavesdropping.The rapid development of IoT brings many applications, such as [4, 39, 50].
Recently, with the rise of wireless sensing technology, voice eavesdropping has received a lot of attention over
the past few decades. Some researchers use vision-based approaches[6, 24] to monitor medium vibrations, such as
foiltogo[6] and light bulb[24], for voice eavesdropping. However, these methods usually depend on the lighting
conditions of environments and can only work in line-of-sight (LOS) situations. Besides vision, the motion sensor-
based voice eavesdropping methods are also explored extensively, such as accelerator[2, 14, 48], gyroscope[22] or
lidar[28], etc. However, the study in work[1] demonstrates that a shared surface that can propagate conductive
vibrations [2, 14, 22] or a high voice volume [ 28, 48] could be required to successfully achieve sensor-based
voice eavesdropping. In addition, some researchers explore utilizing Wi-Fi [38] and mmWave[12, 13]to sense
loudspeaker vibrations for human voice eavesdropping. Tag-Bug[ 34] utilizes RFID to sense nearby objects
vibrations caused by the sound from loudspeaker for voice eavesdropping, but it fails to eavesdrop on live human
speech due to large wind noise. WaveEar[42] leverages mmWave to sense users throat vocal vibrations for voice
eavesdropping, but it needs a customized hardware. In addition, some works, such as [11, 15], utilize microphone
of smartphone to record and recognize the sound, which can be used for voice eavesdropping. However, the
surrounding noise interfere with sound recovery, so it is possible to result in failed voice eavesdropping. In recent
years, some works [37, 43, 47] utilize sound signals to achieve behavior-based sensing, such as finger 3D tracking
[47], toothprint-based user authentication[37], and early recognition of inattentive driving[43]. Although these
works can accurately sense human behavior by sound signals, they cannot achieve voice eavesdropping.
Voice eavesdropping through sensing facial speech dynamics.In recent years, some researchers explore
utilizing facial speech dynamics for voice eavesdropping. Vibphone[31] uses accelerator in smartphone to sense
facial speech dynamics for voice eavesdropping, which needs the physical contact between the smartphone and
human cheek. Face-Mic[30] explores utilizing motion sensors on AR/VR headsets to sense subtle facial dynamics
for inferring highly sensitive information from live human speech, including speaker gender, identity, and speech
content. However, VR headsets are only used in certain situations and require a continuous power supply. Besides,
V-Speech [21] leverages a vibration sensor located in the nasal pads of smart glasses to achieve noise-robust
speech eavesdropping. However, this method requires specially customized smart glasses, which brings a high
cost and limits its application greatly.
Different from these works, RF-Mic leverages a cryptic RFID tag attached on common glasses for live voice
eavesdropping based on RF signals in a natural, low-cost, and robust manner. Moreover,RF-Mic can be applied to
other forms of applications, such as voice eavesdropping or voice assistant in a noisy environment, because it
can recovery human live voice by capturing subtle facial speech dynamics.
9 CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a live voice eavesdropping system, RF-Mic, which utilizes common glasses attached on
a low-cost RFID tag to infer possible voice contents. RF-Mic first pre-processes the collected RF signals. Then,

RF-Mic extracts three kinds of facial speech dynamic features by designing different deep-learning models, and
then fuses the extracted speech dynamic features based on a self-attention method. After that, RF-Mic removes

by utilizing a contrast learning network. Finally, a facial speech dynamics model is constructed for live voice
eavesdropping. Extensive experiments in real environments demonstrate the effectiveness of RF-Mic on human
live voice eavesdropping.
