Abstract—RFID-based mechanical vibration detection is con-
sidered a promising method for many Internet of Things (IoT)
applications. However , existing methods are affected by ambient
interference and leakage from the reader signature, which
makes it difﬁcult to obtain high-precision detection results. T o
overcome this limitation, this paper introduces T agVibra, a high-
precision non-contact vibration sensing system based on RFID
technology. By leveraging the nonlinear effect inside the tag
and the modulation mechanism of RFID, T agVibra can achieve
sub-millimeter level vibration detection accuracy. We implement
T agVibra on our Universal Software Radio Peripheral (USRP)
platform and evaluate its performance via experiments. The
experimental results show that T agVibra can detect vibration
with amplitude as low as 0.3mm, and the average estimation
error below 0.7Hz when the vibration frequency is 100Hz.

I. I NTRODUCTION
With the development of industrial automation, the mechan-
ical vibration detection plays a more and more critical role.
By measuring the mechanical vibration, engineers can not only
identify the working condition of the equipment, but also can
analyze the failure reasons of the equipment [1]. Traditional
vibration detection methods can be roughly divided into two
categories, depending on the sensors used: contact detection
methods and non-contact detection methods. The former is
based on dedicated contact sensors [2] [3], while the latter
mainly relies on lasers or cameras [4] [5]. Although accurate
vibration detection can be achieved, these methods have been
suffering from complex operation, expensive equipment and
difﬁcult maintenance.
RFID-based vibration detection technology has attracted
the attention of researchers in recent years. Tagbeat uses
compressed sensing technology to improve the frequency
range for sensing periodic signals [6], and TagTwins uses a
dual-tag system to reduce the interference of environmental
noise on vibration detection [7]. RED proposes a non-contact
RFID-based method for measuring eccentricity [8]. However,
these two methods are only suitable for measuring mechan-
ical vibrations with large amplitudes. TagSound introduces

capability of RFID systems for minor vibrations [9], and
RF-Ear can distinguish different vibration source devices by
sensing different frequencies [10]. However, in many cases, the

existing measurement methods still need further improvement.
TagSMM can effectively measure vibration signals with an

B. Harmonics
Passive RFID tags do not carry any power source, therefore
they need to collect energy from the reader signal to maintain
their normal functioning. As shown in Fig. 2, in order to

presence of nonlinear effects, when the tag modulates the
carrier signal, in addition to the fundamental frequency, it
will generate a number of components whose frequency are
integers of the fundamental frequency (called harmonics).

A. Harmonic signal receiving
The co-channel leakage from readers is a common problem
in RFID sensing. The high energy leakage deteriorates the
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the RFID tag signal, which
subsequently affects the performance of sensing [15]. TagVibra
receives tag harmonic signals by using a sniffer to avoid
interference from reader leakage and to obtain better vibra-
tion sensing resolution. As shown in Fig. 3, the third-order
harmonic has a better SNR, therefore TagVibra chooses the
third-order harmonic for vibration detection. However, since
the carrier frequency of the reader is not equal to the speciﬁed
frequency, but has a slight offset (i.e., carrier frequency offset,
CFO), receiving harmonic signal directly at 2.7GHz will
inevitably introduce additional phase interference.
Inspired by TagSound [9], TagVibra employs a coarse-to-
ﬁne CFO estimation algorithm. Speciﬁcally, TagVibra ﬁrst
uses FFT to roughly estimate the range of CFO, and then
uses a delay-autocorrelation algorithm to accurately estimate
the CFO using the periodicity of the RFID extended preamble.
After obtaining the estimated CFO, TagVibra eliminates the
impact of CFO by the frequency compensation method.
B. Signal preprocessing
After receiving the harmonic signal, TagVibra ﬁrst uses a
sliding average ﬁlter to denoise the raw signal. Please note that
the harmonic signal received by the sniffer contains both the
reader signal (e.g., Query and ACK) and the tag signal (e.g.,
RN16 and EPC). The vibration signal is only contained in the
tag signal. On the other hand, the EPC has a longer duration
compared to the RN16 and is also more conducive to detect
the vibration status. Therefore TagVibra needs to extract the
EPC from the raw signal for vibration detection.
For computational cost reasons, TagVibra does not imple-
ment RFID reader on the sniffer, so it is unlikely to obtain
the starting and ending positions of EPCs by decoding them.
TagVibra has designed a two-step algorithm to locate the
starting and ending edges of the EPC signal in order to extract
the EPC signal accurately. As shown in Fig. 5(a), the reader
signal has a larger amplitude variation compared to the tag
signal, so TagVibra ﬁrst uses a sliding window and calculates
the variation of the signal within the window to identify the
reader and tag signals.
After removing the reader signal, TagVibra also needs to
extract the EPC signal accurately. Since the amplitude of the
tag signal is larger than noise, TagVibra applies an amplitude-
based threshold to identify the tag signal, as shown in Fig.
5(b). Since RN16 has a shorter duration than EPC, it can be
easily removed from the received signal.
We note that the FM0 encoding used for RFID tags is a
non-zeroing encoding, and using a magnitude-based threshold
to determine the starting position of the EPC may lead to
synchronization errors, but this has a negligible effect on
vibration detection.

applies the Fourier Curve Fitting [18] to estimate the missing
portion of the phase curve. Fig. 8 shows the ﬁtting result in
which the black dots are the measured phase value while the
red curve is the ﬁtted phase curve by using Fourier Curve
Fitting. Obviously, the esitmated curve is well matched with
the measure values. In order to avoid the impact of sudden
interference, a Kalman ﬁlter is used. Finally, the spectrum of
vibration can be obtained easily (as shown in Fig. 9) and the
frequency of vibration can be calculated consequently.

C. Overview performance of vibration sensing
Submillimeter vibration amplitude sensing.We ﬁrst eval-

iment, we ﬁrst observe the phase change obtained by TagVibra
at different amplitudes. We set the vibration frequency to 20Hz
and then gradually adjust the amplitude from 0.9mm to 0.3mm
with the step of 0.2mm. As illustrated in Fig. 11, the amplitude
of the phase curve becomes smaller as the vibration amplitude
decreases, but even when the amplitude is reduced to 0.3mm,
a clear periodic characteristic can still be observed, which
demonstrates that TagVibra has a well-sensing capability for
slight vibrations.
We further analyze the performance of TagVibra in terms
of vibration frequency estimation with different amplitudes.
We set the vibration frequency to 50Hz and set the amplitude
to 0.3mm to 1.5mm with the step of 0.2mm. For each ex-
perimental conﬁguration, we repeat the experiment 100 times
and present the statistical results in Fig. 12. As shown in
the ﬁgure, the estimation error of frequency increases as the
amplitude decreases. When the amplitude is above 0.7mm, the
average error of frequency estimation is below 0.5Hz, while
when the amplitude is less than 0.5mm, the error becomes
signiﬁcantly larger, and when the amplitude is 0.3mm, the
average error has exceeded 1Hz. This may be due to the fact
that when the amplitude is very small, the phase change caused
by the environmental interference is too great, and even after
using the DPA algorithm to amplify the phase, it is difﬁcult
to completely eliminate the interference. It can also be seen
from the phase curves of 0.3mm and 0.5mm in Fig. 11 that
there is a signiﬁcant distortion in the signal of small amplitude
compared to the signal of large amplitude.
Accuracy of vibration frequency estimation.We then test
the frequency estimation performance of TagVibra. We set
the vibration amplitude to 0.7mm and then set the vibration
frequency from 10Hz to 100Hz in steps of 10Hz. For each
of the experimental settings, we repeat the experiment 100
times and present the results in Fig. 13. Although the average
error rate increases as the frequency increases, it stays below
0.7Hz. This indicates that TagVibra has good performance in
frequency estimation.

D. Impact factors
Impact of harmonic on vibration sensing.To verify the
effect of harmonic on vibration detection, an experiment is
conducted. In this experiment, the amplitude is set to 2mm
and the frequency is 20Hz. Then, we collect the backscattered
signals of the tag on the 920MHz and 2.76GHz bands (i.e. the
fundamental and the harmonic), respectively. Then we feed
them into the TagVibra system for processing respectively.
Fig. 14 shows the phase change curves after processing. It
can be seen that for the same vibration amplitude, using the
harmonic signal (blue curve) results in a larger phase change
(from 0.5rad to 1.5rad) and is better for the detection of weak
vibrations.
Impact of distance between tag and vibrator . The
distance between the tag and the vibrator may affect the
performance of TagVibra. In this experiment, we set the
vibration frequency to 20Hz and then observe the change in
TagVibra’s performance by adjusting the distance between the
tag and the vibrator (from 5cm to 50cm in steps of 5cm). For
each experimental conﬁguration, we repeate the experiment
100 times. Fig. 15 shows the result. We can see that the
average error is less than 0.5Hz when the distance is less than
20cm. However, with the increase of distance, the average
error increases signiﬁcantly, when the distance increases to
50cm, the average error is about 1.4Hz. The main reason is
that TagVibra is a non-contact vibration system that relies on
reﬂected signals from the vibrator. When the distance is too
far, the reﬂected signal is too weak to be used for accurate
sensing.
Impact of distance between tag and reader .Next, we
analyze the impact of the distance between the tag to the reader
antenna on the performance of TagVibra. The amplitude and
frequency of vibration are the same as in the previous exper-
iment, and we adjust the distance from the tag to the reader
antenna from 0.3m to 3m, and show the experimental results
on Fig. 16. As we gradually increase the distance from 0.3m
to 3m, the average error also gradually increase. This may
be due to the fact that as the distance increases, the strength
of the reﬂected signal from the tag decreases subsequently,
making the SNR of the phase deteriorate and subsequently
degrading the performance of TagVibra. When the distance
exceeds 2m, it is already close to the communication limit
distance of RFID, at which point the communication anomaly
may also have an impact on the performance of TagVibra.
Impact of distance between tag and sniffer .In this exper-
iment, we set the vibration frequency to 20Hz and gradually
change the distance between sniffer and tag at 10cm intervals.
We conduct 20 experiments at each different distance and
calculate the average error. Fig. 17 shows the results of the
experiment. The implementation results show that when the
distance is less than 30cm, the estimation error of TagVibra is
very small, below 1Hz. As the distance continues to increase,
the error begins to signiﬁcantly increase. When the distance
exceeds 50cm, the error is already very large, making it
difﬁcult to achieve accurate vibration frequency measurement.
And when the distance exceeds 80cm, TagVibra is completely
unable to detect the harmonic scattering signal of the tag, and
it can no longer function normally. This is mainly because,
compared to the fundamental frequency signal, the strength of
the harmonic signal is very weak, and an increase in distance
will cause the harmonic signal received by the monitoring
device to be even weaker. So we should make the sniffer closer
to the tag.

experiment, we mainly evaluate the impact of different tag-
reader angles on the system. We select 5 angles of 0◦,
15◦, 30◦, 45◦, and 60◦, and conduct 50 experiments for

V. DISCUSSION
Through the above experiments, it is worth noting that
distance has a signiﬁcant impact on TagVibra, especially on
the tag-sniffer distance. When the distance exceeds a certain
range, the harmonic cannot be received anymore. Therefore,
if remote vibration measurement is required, we can mainly
consider the following two solutions to enhance signal strength
and then improve system performance. On the one hand, we
can use a RF-ampliﬁer to increase the transmission power. On
the other hand, we can improve the efﬁciency of transmission
and reception by optimizing the design of the antenna. For
example, using a better directional antenna can concentrate
the energy of the signal in a speciﬁc direction.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we have proposed TagVibra, a high-precision
and accurate non-contact vibration frequency inspection sys-
tem. TagVibra is capable of detecting vibrations at different
frequencies with an average error rate of less than 0.7Hz. Ad-
ditionally, we have successfully detected a 0.3mm amplitude
using the harmonic backscattering from the tag, which can be
used to detect mechanical devices failures. In our next plan,
we aim to achieve ﬁner amplitude perception and investigate
quantitative measurement of the vibration amplitude.
